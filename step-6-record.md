# Step 6: Record Your Assessment

Before starting work, make sure the risk assessment you completed in Steps 1–5 is saved in your project's documentation space (see the [Getting Started guide](getting-started.md) for how to set this up). The risk assessment itself is your record — there is no need for a separate log. The purpose is to maintain transparency, support audit requirements, and ensure there is an agreed basis for the work.

**Each risk assessment should capture:**

- **Date** of the assessment
- **Who** is covered by it (this may be one person or several doing the same type of work)
- **What** the AI will be used for (from Step 1)
- **What data** will be shared (from Step 2)
- **Which tool** will be used
- **Inherent risk level** and the individual risk ratings (from Step 3)
- **Mitigations to be applied** (from Step 5)
- **Residual risk level** (from Step 5)
- **Approvals obtained** (if applicable)

After the work is complete, update the assessment with any issues encountered and learnings to share (see [Step 8](step-8-share.md)).

**Periodic review:**

The team should review existing risk assessments at regular intervals — for example, during sprint retrospectives or at monthly team reviews. Use these reviews to:

- Identify patterns: are the same risks coming up repeatedly? Are mitigations working?
- Share learnings: what has worked well? What should be avoided?
- Update risk assessments: has the risk profile changed as the team has gained experience?
- Check for drift: are team members following the framework, or has usage become informal?
- Assess whether the tools and approaches are still appropriate as the project evolves
- Check for staleness: have any assessments become outdated because the scope, data, or tooling has changed?

Your collection of risk assessments also supports wider governance requirements. If the project is subject to audit, the assessments provide evidence of responsible AI use. If an incident occurs, they support investigation.
